## What is Grok Bot?

Two products share the name Grok, and they are not the same thing. The **Grok chatbot** (at grok.com) is xAI's general-purpose AI assistant, a ChatGPT competitor. **Grok Bot** is the other one, and that is what I am comparing here. Learn more about the AI employee category in [What is an AI Employee](https://www.marblism.com/blog/what-is-an-ai-employee).

[Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot) launched on August 11, 2026, in early beta as a joint product from xAI and Cursor. It is an always-on multi-agent platform where you create named "Bots," each assigned its own persistent cloud computer. Each Bot signs into real apps and websites on your behalf, browses, sends email, and coordinates with other Bots. In practice: a named agent with a desktop that stays logged in and never stops working. Worth knowing from the start: the launch post states that Grok Bot "comes with its own usage, separate from your Grok and Cursor plans."

The catch is familiar: access rides on an existing subscription, not a standalone purchase, and there is no free tier. The launch post lists eight eligible plans across xAI and Cursor.

Grok Bot access: eligible plans and prices at launch

xAI's Business and Enterprise plans do not include Grok Bot. Enterprise users can join a waitlist for future access.

There is one more thing worth knowing before you sign up: [at launch](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/11/grok-bot-macos-ios/), all Bots under a single account share one cloud computer, one set of files, and one set of browser sessions. That is the part the [launch-day discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49261514) kept coming back to, second only to the cost.

### What is the Grok Bot marketplace?

The Grok Bot marketplace is where users browse and install bots other people have built. Each bot is a saved routine with its own instructions and workflows that runs on your account's shared cloud computer. The closest equivalents among alternatives on this list are OpenClaw's ClawHub, a community marketplace for third-party skills, Hermes Agent's Skills Hub (90,000+ installable skills across 11 registries), and Lindy's browsable use-case catalog. Neither Buzz nor Marblism publishes one on its own site.

## What Grok Bot alternatives already offer that Grok Bot does not

Grok Bot launched in August 2026 and is still in early beta. Most tools below have years of user feedback, established pricing, and compliance features that are live right now. Here is what that track record buys you.

### Every alternative on this list has a billing cap; Grok Bot does not

The top comment on Grok Bot's 348-point Hacker News launch thread came from [jjcm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49263241): "Biggest downsides are token expenditure. I've used more tokens this month than not this month. That's not a typo - I've used less tokens in the last 5 years prior to this month than I have this month. Always on perpetual agents use a LOT of tokens."

That is not a fringe complaint. Usage runs on a separate meter and no spend cap is published anywhere on xAI's surfaces. Early reviewer accounts report a cap as a coming feature, not a current one. Every tool below charges a flat rate or publishes its per-operation limits upfront. You can price it before you commit.

### The cheapest way to access Grok Bot, and the free alternatives

Three alternatives run completely free right now: Hermes Agent (MIT license), Buzz (Apache 2.0), and OpenClaw (MIT license). All three are open-source and self-hosted: you provide the model and the hardware. Viktor also offers a free tier with up to $100 in credits before you pay anything.

The cheapest paid route into Grok Bot is Cursor Pro at [$20 a month](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot). SuperGrok at $30 a month is the next option up. Both include weekly Grok Bot usage, but additional usage is billed based on token cost with no published allowance and no way to cap spend. The $0 Grok plan on xAI's pricing page explicitly excludes Grok Bot: there is no free route in.

### Grok Bot launched in August 2026 and is still in early beta; the alternatives below have real track records

Early reviewer Aditi Gupta, writing on DEV Community after hands-on time with Grok Bot, found that "it got stuck on simple clicks, brought back half the data, and sometimes just froze on easy tasks." Beta tools have rough edges. Paying from $20 a month plus an uncapped usage meter for one that warns you to double-check its sources is a steep price for an experiment that is still running.

### Your credentials are not stored on a shared cloud computer

At launch, every Bot under one Grok Bot account shares the same cloud computer: same files, same browser sessions, same logins. Per-Bot credential isolation is on the roadmap, not shipped. If that separation matters to you, it does not exist yet.

### Self-hosted alternatives run on hardware you control

Grok Bot runs on xAI's cloud infrastructure. Hermes Agent, Buzz, and OpenClaw all run on your own machine, with your own data and your own model provider. You own the stack entirely. Every cloud-based alternative on this list, Marblism, Manus, Perplexity Computer, Viktor, Lindy, Claude Cowork, and ChatGPT Work, is web-based and works on any operating system.

### Several alternatives already ship audit trails and spending controls

Early reviewer accounts note that Grok Bot's audit log is listed as coming, not shipped today. Viktor ships with SOC 2 and GDPR compliance. Lindy offers SOC 2 and HIPAA at Enterprise tier. Marblism requires human-in-the-loop approval before any consequential action goes out: sending email, posting publicly, signing a contract, or making an outbound call.

If you want to own the infrastructure, avoid subscriptions, and pick your own model provider, three alternatives are free and open-source.
